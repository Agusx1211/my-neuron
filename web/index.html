      How it works

      The model. Qwen3.5-4B, a real 4-billion-parameter language model, is downloaded once and runs on your GPU through WebGPU. No server sees your text.

      The neurons. Each of its 32 layers has an MLP with 9,216 neurons, 294,912 in total. A neuron's activation is silu(gate)·up, the SwiGLU value right before the down-projection. Every disk in the tower is one layer; every dot is one neuron.

      Finding yours. We run your text through the model and read every neuron at every token (small words like “my” or “the” are skipped). Each neuron is compared to how it fires across 17,720 concepts (people, places, foods, feelings, names, internet handles), measured offline with the same weights. A neuron qualifies when your text would land in its own top list, and it wins when it fires far past its normal range (in standard deviations, σ) and its favorite concepts are about the same thing you typed, judged by the model's own internal representations.

      Two poles. SwiGLU neurons can fire strongly positive or strongly negative, and both sides tend to mean something. Amber is the positive pole, blue the negative one.

      What else lights it up. A precomputed atlas lists the concepts that drive each neuron hardest. When you open it, the model in your browser re-measures them live.

      Privacy. Your text is only ever processed on your own GPU. We use Google Analytics to count anonymous usage (page views, which model loads, load and analysis times), never what you type.

      Inspired by Goodfire's “Neural Networks Are Already Clean”, which argues that individual neurons are far more interpretable than commonly believed.
